An acceptance criterion describes an observable condition that meets a user need. “Add Redis” names an implementation. It does not establish how fresh, fast or private the resulting dashboard must be.

Specify actor, initial state, trigger and expected observation. For this synthetic task: after an accepted source change, that account’s dashboard must show the change within 60 seconds under the stated test conditions; the sampled response must be under 1000ms; no other account’s rows may appear. These are exercise thresholds, not universal product recommendations.

Evaluate the dimensions separately. A very fast stale response can fail freshness. A fresh cross-user response fails isolation. A failure state also needs a criterion: for example, show a recoverable error rather than silently presenting an empty result as current data.

A single sample does not establish a p95 or service-level objective. Define a representative window and conditions before making such a claim. Passing acceptance checks supports the specified behaviour; product success also needs evidence that the outcome helps users.
